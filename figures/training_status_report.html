Contact-Map Arms Race

tri_mul leads by 2.3× on full-trajectory sampling from a quarter of the training steps. It is running again after an outage traced to a one-line defect in our own checkpoint-corruption fix. Two new warm-started arms now sit beside it — a fused-Gromov-Wasserstein alignment head and its matched control — both carrying a new distogram head that trains at full weight. local_attn stays stopped after a degradation that survived every mechanism we could test.

tri — validation P@L: one-step (by noise level) and full sampling

Two different questions. One-step P@L asks: given a contact map corrupted to noise level t, how well does a single denoising step recover the true contacts? Sampling P@L asks the question that actually matters at deployment: run the full generative rollout from noise and score the contact map it produces. P@L = precision over the top-L predicted contacts, L = chain length. x-axis is epoch, not step — trainer/global_step resets on every resume and this run has eight of them, so it is not a usable axis.

How to read helix_pos_frac — low is correct. It is the fraction of helical-range CA pseudo-dihedrals that are positive, and it is a handedness indicator, not a quality score. Calibrated on 254 native chains: median 0.0815, mean 0.1225, only 2.0% above 0.5. So ~0.12 is native-handed and ~0.89 is mirrored. The model now reads 0.096 — inside the native band. The fall from 0.599 at step 5,431 to under 0.13 is the fold flipping to the correct hand.

⛔ Two traps this page deliberately does not fall into. (1) chirality_agree is not a mirror detector despite the name — it tests per-residue stereocentres, and mirrored generations keep their residues correctly L (measured 0.999 across 122 mirrored chains), so it reports “all good” through a 48% mirror rate. (2) helix_pos_frac is a local statistic — the same quantity the chirality loss trains on — so it cannot independently confirm a chirality fix. The non-circular discriminator is the proper-vs-reflected superposition gap.

Why is_mirrored is not plotted as a rate. It fires only when proper > 2×reflected and the gap exceeds 1 Å, so it is fit-quality-biased: before ~step 3,000 the generated structures sit 11.6–145 Å from native, where the test cannot meaningfully fire and a reading of 0 means no verdict, not “correct hand”. It last read non-zero at step 9,219 and has been 0 at every validation round since. ⛔ Per-round values are a liveness monitor over ~16 structures, not a rate: 0 of 16 is entirely consistent with the ~1.6% rate measured by the 512-draw paired assay (P = 0.9816 ≈ 0.77).

tri’s contact_precision_at_L is deliberately absent. The same quantity reads 0.0225 to 1.000 depending on noise level within one validation pass, because these are single-step loss-path snapshots rather than the sampled metric. Plotting one would be a meaningless curve. The comparable sampled number comes only from tri_gen_eval.py.

Earlier — 4 Sep 2026: two new arms, and what makes them readable

Everything from here down is the 4 Sep 2026 report on tri_full384 and localattn_full384. Those are DIFFERENT arms from the two live models above (tri_cb8synth_v5, c2c_cb8_tbeta); it is kept for the reasoning, not as current status.

Both warm-start from the same pinned, immutable checkpoint — tri's best by its own configured selection metric, contact_precision_at_L_median = 0.6780 at epoch 104. That number came from reading the ModelCheckpoint callback state rather than picking by filename; the mean-best sits at epoch 110 instead, so choosing by the wrong metric would have warm-started the wrong weights.

The difference is 41,729 parameters — exactly the head. That matters because the warm start perturbs three things at once: EMA-vs-live weights, a fresh optimizer, and the head. Against a ±0.02–0.04 resolution floor, “fgw helped” and “the restart helped” are not separable from a single arm. The control eats the same perturbation, so subtracting it isolates the coupling. No fgw claim should be read until it has run.

Both also carry a new distogram head that trains at full weight — a 39-bin cross-entropy at parity with the contact loss for every sample above t = 0.3. Attaching it required fixing a latent trap: the trainer tests predict_coords is False by identity, and tri was the only arm setting False, so the instant a distogram existed tri alone would have entered a structure-prediction path whose two backends both raise NotImplementedError — one of which has no class definition anywhere in the repo. Normalising it to None, as the other two arms already did, defuses it.

Gates before either arm reached a GPU: the fgw warm start loads 498 keys with 7 missing, all of them ot_align.*, and 0 unexpected; the control loads with 0 missing and 0 unexpected; the distogram head passes 14/14 checks including contact output bit-identical with and without the head.

Why tri is off the GPUs: 82 characters

tri hit a disk-quota wall at 21:35 on 2 Sep. The relaunch and all four of its self-chained successors then died in about six minutes each, burning the entire chain between 02:02 and 02:28. The cause is not the quota, and it is not the model — it is the fix we shipped for the checkpoint-corruption bug.

That fix pointed TMPDIR at the checkpoints' own directory so Lightning's atomic rename() would stop being a cross-device copy. But PyTorch hands DataLoader-worker tensors to the parent through an AF UNIX socket created under $TMPDIR, and sockaddr_un.sun_path is 108 bytes. Python appends a 32-character socket name, so the new 82-character temp dir overflowed it on every batch handoff.

armTMPDIRlen + 32result
as shipped$REPO/store/$RUN/checkpoints/.tmp82 + 32 = 114 OSError: AF_UNIX path too long → hangs
repaired/orcd/scratch/…/chenxiou/.tmp36 + 32 = 68 batches delivered, exit 0, 7 s

The dangerous part is that it hangs rather than crashes. The failure happens in the worker's feeder thread, so batches simply never arrive and the log shows a stall, never a path error. That is exactly the signature the 48M production run has been stuck on since July — DataLoader timed out after 300 seconds on the first batch, with cold cache, bad node, slow data, worker count and host memory all eliminated by measurement. Its launchers carry the same pattern at 113 characters, already over the limit before the socket name is appended, so that path could never have worked. Both blockers trace to the same commit.

Repaired in all three launchers, with a fail-fast guard so it cannot recur silently. Atomicity is preserved — the new temp dir is device 208 on fstor018.ib:/, the same device as every checkpoint directory it serves, verified with stat -c '%d'. Nothing was lost: last.ckpt and last-EMA.ckpt are intact at step 45,500, and the resume itself was healthy — it restored cleanly and produced tri's best sampling numbers on record before the dataloader took it down.

local_attn: four hypotheses, all eliminated

The degradation is real and lives in the weights — it reproduces offline, −0.079 precision@L on the identical 32 chains, two to four times the noise band. What it is not is now settled by measurement rather than argument.

What remains is the residual claim itself. The per-step map ended at its all-time best — train single-step precision@L 0.8748 at epoch 434, validation loss 0.0587 at epoch 412, both records set after the collapse — while its 50-step composition got worse, and worst at long range. That is a statement about the iterated operator, not about any component of it.

The self-conditioning test is worth stating plainly because the prediction was written down first and it failed: if self-conditioning carried the damage, turning it off should have shrunk the gap. It did not. Self-conditioning is doing real work (+0.11 at both checkpoints) — that reliance simply never changed.

Training was stopped at epoch 466. No further training was expected to help, and every remaining test works from checkpoints.

Does the trunk already know the alignment? Mostly yes — and it is mostly learned

We were about to add an optimal-transport head so the model could align the query onto its topology reference, because the current encoding is a crude clipped left-alignment. Before building it, we asked whether the model needed it: a Linear(320 → 1) probe on frozen trunk features, predicting a USalign ground truth, scored by precision@Q against a floor that sees only the positional offset.

The floor is the whole experiment — left-alignment is a decent prior, so a probe scored against chance would look spectacular and mean nothing. And one more arm turned out to be essential: the same pipeline with no checkpoint loaded at all, because a linear probe on random features is not nothing either.

precision@Q, 5-fold over held-out chains — 107 distinct (query, reference) pairs, split by chain
Trunkfloorprobeprobe − floor
untrained (random init)0.39750.4897+0.092
trained (epoch 105)0.37070.6966+0.326

The learned component is +0.234 — the trained gap is ~3.5× the untrained one, well outside the combined spreads. The trunk genuinely acquires alignment information through training. But roughly 28% of the raw gap is architectural, not learned, which an earlier version of this page attributed entirely to learning. That correction is why the control was worth running.

The head does not overfit, and that is measured rather than assumed. Across every fold and every arm the largest train-minus-test gap in precision@Q is +0.012, against fold spreads of 0.05–0.12; for the trained probe the test score is fractionally better than train. The head is 321 parameters against ~620,000 cells. Deduplicating the 43 pairs that the trainer's self-conditioning double-forward captured twice moves the result only from +0.343 to +0.326.

This still weakens the case for the OT head rather than strengthening it. The model is not failing to realign. The open question is whether an explicit coupling does it better or cheaper — a materially different, and weaker, motivation than the original design assumed.

Two caveats kept in view. The two arms draw their own chains, visible in floors that should be equal (0.405 vs 0.351); chain-set variation moves the floor ~0.05 against a 0.25 difference in the gap, so it does not change the conclusion, but a fixed chain list would make them paired. And this measures decodability from frozen features, not that the sampler uses the alignment.

One in five training samples is trivially satisfiable

While building the probe we measured how often a sample receives its own topology as the reference. Design assumption: 6.66%. Measured through the real dataloader: 20.5% on train, 19.3% on val.

Three inconsistent numbers took four experiments to reconcile, and the first explanation — ours — was wrong. The cause is not a bug in the reference picker, which is exactly correct (measured self-rate 1.667%, matching its closed form). It is sampling_mode: cluster-random: it draws one chain per cluster, so a chain alone in its cluster is seen every epoch while one in a 500-member cluster is seen 1/500 as often — and "alone in its cluster" is precisely "no different-sequence mate to use as a reference".

PopulationRateIs this what training sees?
per chain (306,749 chains)1.92%no
per cluster (3,121 of 14,885)20.97%yes
measured through the dataloader19.3–20.7%—

The mechanism is benign — sampling geometry, not a defect — but the consequence stands: roughly one conditioned sample in five is trivially satisfiable, for both arms. The standing lesson is that a per-chain statistic does not describe what a cluster-sampled run actually trains on.
